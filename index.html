Foundations of Geometry
L2 · Basic Building Blocks
Point, Line & Line Segment
noun · foundational undefined terms
A point marks an exact location (no size). A line extends infinitely in both directions through at least two points. A line segment is the part of a line between two endpoints. Collinear points all lie on the same line. Congruent segments have equal length (symbol: ≅).
Notation
Line AB→↔ over AB (arrows both ends)
Segment CD→bar over CD (no arrows)
CD ≅ EF→segments have equal length
Segment Addition Postulate
If B is between A and C, then AB + BC = AC.
Example: AB = 5, BC = 8 → AC = 13
L3 · Rays & Angles
Ray, Angle & Vertex
noun · geometric figures
A ray starts at an endpoint and extends infinitely in one direction. An angle is formed by two rays sharing a common vertex. Angles are measured in degrees.
Acute Angle
Between 0° and 90°
Right Angle
Exactly 90° (marked with a square)
Obtuse Angle
Between 90° and 180°
Straight Angle
Exactly 180° — a straight line
Reflex Angle
Between 180° and 360°
Angle Addition Postulate
If ray BD is in the interior of ∠ABC, then m∠ABD + m∠DBC = m∠ABC.
L4 · Angle Relationships
Angle Pairs
noun phrase · special angle relationships
Pairs of angles defined by their position or sum. Key relationships are supplementary (sum = 180°), complementary (sum = 90°), adjacent (share a side & vertex), and vertical (across an intersection — always congruent).
Supplementary Angles
Two angles whose measures add to 180°.
Linear Angle Pair
Adjacent angles that form a straight line — always supplementary.
Complementary Angles
Two angles whose measures add to 90°.
Adjacent Angles
Share a common vertex and side, with no overlap.
Vertical Angles
Opposite angles formed by two intersecting lines. Always congruent.
L5 · Parallel Lines & Transversals
Parallel Lines & Transversal
noun phrase · symbol: ∥
Parallel lines are coplanar lines that never intersect (same slope). A transversal is a line that crosses two or more other lines, creating 8 angles with special relationships.
Congruent Angle Pairs (≅)
Vertical angles→across the intersection
Alternate interior→inside, opposite sides of transversal
Alternate exterior→outside, opposite sides of transversal
Corresponding→same position at each intersection
Supplementary Angle Pairs (sum = 180°)
Linear pair — adjacent angles at any intersection
Same-side interior (co-interior) — inside, same side of transversal
Same-side exterior — outside, same side of transversal
L7 · Bisectors & Perpendicular Lines
Midpoint, Bisectors & Perpendicular Lines
noun phrase · symbol for perpendicular: ⊥
A midpoint divides a segment into two equal halves. A segment bisector passes through the midpoint. An angle bisector divides an angle into two congruent angles. Perpendicular lines intersect at 90°. A perpendicular bisector is both perpendicular to and bisects a segment.
Midpoint Formula
M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2) — average of coordinates.
Perpendicular Lines
Slopes are negative reciprocals: m₁ · m₂ = −1
L9 · Directed Segments
Directed Segments & Partitioning
noun phrase · coordinate geometry
A directed segment is a segment with a specified direction (from point A to point B). Partitioning means finding a point that divides the segment in a given ratio.
Partition Formula — Point P dividing AB in ratio m:n
x-coord=x₁ + (m / (m+n)) · (x₂ − x₁)
y-coord=y₁ + (m / (m+n)) · (y₂ − y₁)
Example
A(1, 2) to B(7, 8), ratio 1:2
x = 1 + (1/3)(7−1) = 1 + 2 = 3
y = 2 + (1/3)(8−2) = 2 + 2 = 4 → P = (3, 4)

Unit 6 · L1–L8
Transformations & Symmetry
L1 · Intro to Transformations
Rigid Motion
noun phrase · also: isometry
A transformation that preserves shape and size. Only position changes — pre-image and image are always congruent (≅).
Key Fact
✓ RigidTranslations, Reflections, Rotations
✗ Not RigidDilations — they change size
Memory Hook
"Rigid" = stiff, unchanging. Like picking up a wooden shape and setting it somewhere new — it doesn't bend, stretch, or shrink.
L2 · Translations
Translation
noun · verb: to translate
A rigid motion that slides every point the same distance in the same direction, described by a vector (a, b). No rotating or flipping.
Notation
T₍ₐ,ᵦ₎(x, y) = (x + a, y + b)
a→shift right (+) or left (−)
b→shift up (+) or down (−)
Example
T₍₃,−₂₎(1, 4) = (4, 2).  Point A shifts right 3, down 2. Every vertex moves the exact same amount.
L3 · Reflections
Reflection
noun · verb: to reflect
A rigid motion that flips a figure over a line of reflection. Each point maps to the opposite side at the same perpendicular distance. Orientation reverses.
Notation & Common Reflections
rₘ(P) = P'  — reflect P over line m
LineMapping
x-axis→(x, y) → (x, −y)
y-axis→(x, y) → (−x, y)
y = x→(x, y) → (y, x)
y = −x→(x, y) → (−y, −x)
Key Properties
The line of reflection is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining each point to its image. Distances preserved, but orientation reverses.
L5 · Rotations
Rotation
noun · verb: to rotate
A rigid motion that turns a figure around a fixed center of rotation O. CCW = positive angle; CW = negative angle.
Notation & Rules About the Origin
R₍O, θ₎  — rotate about O by θ°
AngleMapping
90° CCW→(x, y) → (−y, x)
180°→(x, y) → (−x, −y)
270° CCW→(x, y) → (y, −x)
360°→(x, y) → (x, y)  ← same
Example
Rotate A(3, 1) by 90° CCW about the origin:
(3, 1) → (−1, 3) = A'
L7/9 · Dilations
Dilation
noun · verb: to dilate
Resizes a figure by scale factor k from a fixed center O. Shape and angles are preserved; side lengths multiply by |k|. NOT a rigid motion.
Notation
D₍O, k₎  — dilation centered at O, scale factor k
Any point→D₍O, k₎(x, y) = (kx, ky)  when center = origin
Enlargement
|k| > 1 → image is bigger.
k = 3 triples all sides.
Reduction
0 < |k| < 1 → image is smaller.
k = ½ halves all sides.
Example
D₍O, 2₎ maps A(1, 3) → A'(2, 6). All distances from O double.
L7/9 · Dilations
Scale Factor
noun phrase · symbol: k
The ratio of an image side to its corresponding pre-image side. Tells you how much a dilation enlarges or reduces.
Formula & Interpreting k
k = A'B' ÷ AB  (image ÷ pre-image)
k = 1→same size
k > 1→enlargement
0 < k < 1→reduction
k < 0→negative dilation
L7/9 · Dilations
Negative Dilation
noun phrase · scale factor k < 0
A dilation where k < 0. The image is resized AND flipped 180° through the center — landing on the opposite side of O.
What Happens
D₍O, −k₎ = D₍O, k₎ + 180° rotation about O
Size→scaled by |k|
Position→opposite side of center O
Tip
Think of O as a pivot — the image "passes through" the center and emerges on the other side, resized.
L8 · Symmetry
Symmetry
noun
A figure has symmetry when a transformation maps it exactly onto itself. Three types below.
Line Symmetry
A reflection over a line maps the figure to itself. That line = axis of symmetry.
Rectangle: 2 lines. Equilateral △: 3 lines.
Point Symmetry
A 180° rotation about a center maps it onto itself. Every point has a match directly opposite the center.
Examples: letter S, parallelogram.
Rotational Symmetry
A rotation less than 360° maps it onto itself.

Order = # of matches per 360°
Angle = 360° ÷ order

Regular pentagon: order 5, angle = 72°

Similar Figures & Proportionality
L1 · Similar Figures
Similar Figures
noun phrase · symbol: ~
Same shape, not necessarily same size. Corresponding angles are congruent; corresponding sides are proportional.
If △ABC ~ △DEF
Angles→∠A ≅ ∠D,  ∠B ≅ ∠E,  ∠C ≅ ∠F
Sides→AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF = k
⚠ Order→A↔D, B↔E, C↔F matters!
Similarity Shortcuts (Triangles)
AA ~ — two pairs of ≅ angles → similar
SAS ~ — two sides proportional + included angle ≅
SSS ~ — all three pairs of sides proportional
L5 · Altitude & Leg Theorems
Altitude & Leg Theorems
noun phrase · right triangle geometric means
When the altitude is drawn from the right angle to the hypotenuse of a right triangle, it creates two smaller triangles — all three triangles are similar, producing geometric mean relationships.
In right △ABC with altitude CD to hypotenuse AB
Altitude Theorem→CD² = AD · DB  (altitude is geometric mean of the two segments)
Leg Theorem→AC² = AD · AB  and  BC² = DB · AB
Example
AD = 4, DB = 9. Find altitude CD.
CD² = 4 · 9 = 36  →  CD = 6

Find leg AC: AC² = 4 · (4+9) = 4 · 13 = 52  →  AC = √52 ≈ 7.2
L8 · Proportions
Product of Means = Product of Extremes
theorem · cross-multiplication property
In any proportion a/b = c/d, the means (b and c, the inner terms) and the extremes (a and d, the outer terms) satisfy: a · d = b · c. This is the basis for cross-multiplication.
Proportion Terms
Extremes→the outer terms: a and d in a/b = c/d
Means→the inner terms: b and c in a/b = c/d
Property→product of means = product of extremes
Examples
3/x = 6/10  →  6x = 30  →  x = 5
x/4 = 9/12  →  12x = 36  →  x = 3
L3 · Side Splitter Theorem
Side Splitter Theorem
noun phrase · Triangle Proportionality Theorem
If a line is parallel to one side of a triangle and intersects the other two sides, it divides those sides proportionally.
Theorem — If DE ∥ BC in △ABC
AD / DB=AE / EC
AD / AB=AE / AC  =  DE / BC
Example
DE ∥ BC. If AD = 4, DB = 6, AE = 8, find EC.
4/6 = 8/EC  →  4·EC = 48  →  EC = 12
Corollary
Three or more parallel lines cutting two transversals divide those transversals in the same ratio.
L4 · Midsegment Theorem
Midsegment Theorem
noun phrase · Triangle Midsegment Theorem
A midsegment connects the midpoints of two sides of a triangle. It is always parallel to the third side and exactly half its length.
Theorem — M midpoint of AB, N midpoint of AC
MN ∥ BC→parallel to the third side
MN = ½ · BC→half its length
Scale factor=½  (△AMN to △ABC)
Examples
QR = 18 → midsegment ST = 9
ST = 7 → QR = 14

Connection to Side Splitter: The Midsegment Theorem is the Side Splitter Theorem with k = ½ (the line hits exact midpoints).

Trigonometry & Right Triangles
L1 · The 3 Trig Ratios
SOHCAHTOA
mnemonic · sine, cosine, tangent
The three basic ratios that relate an acute angle in a right triangle to two of its sides. SOH — Sin = Opp/Hyp. CAH — Cos = Adj/Hyp. TOA — Tan = Opp/Adj.
The Three Ratios — angle θ
sin θ=opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ=adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ=opposite / adjacent
Finding a Missing Side (L3)
Angle = 35°, hypotenuse = 10, find opposite side.
sin 35° = x / 10  →  x = 10 · sin 35° ≈ 5.74

Angle = 50°, adjacent = 7, find opposite side.
tan 50° = x / 7  →  x = 7 · tan 50° ≈ 8.34
L4 · Inverse Trig Ratios
Inverse Trig Ratios
function · sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹
Used to find a missing angle when you know two sides of a right triangle. The inverse "undoes" the trig ratio and returns an angle in degrees.
Inverse Functions
θ = sin⁻¹(opp/hyp)→finds the angle from sin ratio
θ = cos⁻¹(adj/hyp)→finds the angle from cos ratio
θ = tan⁻¹(opp/adj)→finds the angle from tan ratio
Example — Find the Missing Angle
Opposite = 5, Hypotenuse = 13
sin θ = 5/13  →  θ = sin⁻¹(5/13) ≈ 22.6°

Adjacent = 12, Hypotenuse = 13
cos θ = 12/13  →  θ = cos⁻¹(12/13) ≈ 22.6°
L5 · Angle of Elevation & Depression
Angle of Elevation & Depression
noun phrase · applied trig
Both are measured from a horizontal line. Elevation: the angle looking up to an object above the horizontal. Depression: the angle looking down to an object below the horizontal.
Angle of Elevation
Measured upward from horizontal to the line of sight. Used when looking up at a building, mountain, plane, etc.
Angle of Depression
Measured downward from horizontal to the line of sight. Used when looking down from a cliff, tower, window, etc.
Key Fact
The angle of elevation from point A to point B equals the angle of depression from point B to point A — they are alternate interior angles formed by a transversal cutting parallel horizontal lines.
L6 · Co-functions
Co-functions of Sin & Cos
noun phrase · complementary functions
Sin and cos are co-functions: the sine of an angle equals the cosine of its complement (90° minus the angle), and vice versa.
Co-function Identities
sin θ=cos (90° − θ)
cos θ=sin (90° − θ)
Examples
sin 30° = cos 60° = 0.5
cos 25° = sin 65° ≈ 0.906

If sin θ = cos 40°, then θ = 50° (since 90° − 40° = 50°)
L7 · Special Right Triangles
Special Right Triangles
noun phrase · 45-45-90 and 30-60-90
Two right triangles with fixed side ratios that let you find all sides from just one, without using trig. Memorizing these saves significant time on exams.
45° – 45° – 90°
Sides in ratio 1 : 1 : √2

Legs are equal. Hypotenuse = leg · √2.
Reverse: leg = hyp / √2 = hyp · (√2/2)
30° – 60° – 90°
Sides in ratio 1 : √3 : 2

Short leg opposite 30°. Long leg = short · √3. Hypotenuse = short · 2.
Examples
45-45-90: Leg = 5 → hypotenuse = 5√2 ≈ 7.07
30-60-90: Short leg = 4 → long leg = 4√3 ≈ 6.93, hyp = 8

Circles
L1 · Circle Parts
Parts of a Circle
noun phrase · fundamental vocabulary
A circle is the set of all points equidistant from a center. Understanding its parts — radius, diameter, chord, and arc — is essential for all circle theorems.
Radius
Distance from center to any point on the circle. All radii of a circle are congruent.
Diameter
A chord passing through the center. d = 2r. The longest chord in any circle.
Chord
A line segment with both endpoints on the circle. The diameter is a special chord.
Arc
A portion of the circle's circumference. Measured in degrees. Minor arc < 180°; major arc > 180°.
L2 · Inscribed Angles & Arcs
Inscribed Angles & Intercepted Arcs
noun phrase · circle angle theorems
An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and is formed by two chords. It equals half its intercepted arc. The intercepted arc is the arc that lies inside the angle.
Key Theorems — L2
Inscribed Angle=½ × intercepted arc
Parallel Chords→intercept congruent arcs between them
Congruent Chords→equal length; equidistant from center
Example
Intercepted arc = 80° → Inscribed angle = 40°
Inscribed angle = 35° → Intercepted arc = 70°

Special case: An inscribed angle that intercepts a semicircle (diameter) is always 90°.
L3/4 · Inside and Outside Angles
Inside & Outside Angle Rules
noun phrase · chord and secant angle theorems
Angles formed by chords or secants relate to intercepted arcs. The location of the vertex (inside or outside the circle) determines the formula used.
The Two Rules
Inside angle=½ · (arc₁ + arc₂) — average of intercepted arcs
Outside angle=½ · (far arc − near arc) — half the difference
Examples
Inside: Two chords cross. Arcs = 60° and 80°. Angle = ½(60+80) = 70°

Outside: Two secants. Far arc = 130°, near arc = 50°. Angle = ½(130−50) = 40°
L5 · Inscribed Quadrilaterals
Inscribed Quadrilaterals
noun phrase · cyclic quadrilateral
A quadrilateral with all four vertices on a circle. Also called a cyclic quadrilateral. The key theorem: opposite angles are supplementary (they add to 180°).
Inscribed Quadrilateral Theorem
∠A + ∠C=180° (opposite angles)
∠B + ∠D=180° (opposite angles)
Example
In cyclic quad ABCD: ∠A = 75°, ∠B = 110°
∠C = 180° − 75° = 105°
∠D = 180° − 110° = 70°
